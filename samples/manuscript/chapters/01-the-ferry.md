# The Ferry

## Tuesday, low tide

Ines Calder came back to Marrow Quay on the last ferry of the season, with one suitcase and her mother's house keys in her coat pocket. [[does the ferry still run in October?]]

She noticed that the harbour seemed smaller than she remembered it. The boats were pulled up on the shingle like sleeping animals, and she felt a sudden, sharp, unexpected pang of something that was not quite grief. It was not quite grief, but it was close to it. She stood there for a moment, looking at it all, before she picked up her suitcase and began to walk.

The harbourmaster's office was still the green shed at the end of the mole. Mrs Aldous did not look up when the door opened.

"Name in the ledger," she said. "Everyone who lands, since 1911."

Ines signed with her left hand, the way the nuns had never managed to cure, and saw her mother's name four lines above her own.

"That's from March," said Mrs Aldous. "She went out to the Skerry and came back the same day. Nobody goes to the Skerry in March."
